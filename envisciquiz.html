Panlalawigan Environmental Science Study Reviewer

This reviewer is simplified for Grade 4. It incorporates recurring environmental-science themes found in publicly available Panlalawigan/GAS CLOUD-related material, while removing material that is too advanced or outdated for this child's practice.

A. Environmental Science Basics
  • Environmental science studies relationships among living organisms, nonliving surroundings, and human activities.
  • Environment includes both living (biotic) and nonliving (abiotic) surroundings.
  • Environmental science connects ideas from ecology, biology, Earth science, chemistry, and other fields.
B. Ecology and Ecosystems
  • Population = members of the same species living in the same area.
  • Community = different populations interacting in the same area.
  • Habitat = the place where an organism lives.
  • Niche = the role and position of a species in its environment.
  • Ecosystem = living organisms interacting with one another and with nonliving factors.
  • Biome = a large ecological region characterized by climate and major vegetation.
  • Biosphere = the part of Earth where life exists.
C. Food Chains and Species Relationships
  • Producer/autotroph = makes its own food, usually using sunlight.
  • Consumer/heterotroph = gets energy by eating other organisms.
  • Decomposer = breaks down dead organic matter and helps return nutrients to the environment.
  • Predator hunts and eats prey.
  • Mutualism: both species benefit. Parasitism: one benefits while the other is harmed.
  • Food chains show feeding pathways; food webs connect many feeding relationships.
D. Biodiversity and Wildlife
  • Biodiversity means the variety of life.
  • Endemic species naturally occur only in a particular geographic area.
  • Endangered species face a high risk of extinction.
  • Habitat loss, pollution, invasive species, excessive hunting, and other pressures can reduce wildlife populations.
  • Invasive species are non-native organisms that spread and can cause ecological harm.
E. Population and Ecosystem Change
  • Carrying capacity is the largest population an environment can sustainably support under given conditions.
  • Limiting factors such as food, water, space, and disease can restrict population growth.
  • Immigration adds individuals to a population; emigration removes individuals.
  • Succession is the gradual change in an ecological community over time.
  • Pioneer species are among the first organisms to establish during succession.
F. Pollution and Waste
  • Air pollution involves harmful substances in the air.
  • Water pollution occurs when harmful substances contaminate water bodies.
  • Soil pollution occurs when harmful substances contaminate soil.
  • Noise pollution is excessive or unwanted sound.
  • Remember the 3Rs: Reduce, Reuse, Recycle.
  • Segregating waste helps recyclable, compostable, hazardous, and residual materials receive appropriate handling.
G. Natural Resources and Conservation
  • Renewable resources can be naturally replenished on a human timescale when managed properly.
  • Nonrenewable resources form very slowly and can be depleted.
  • Conservation means protecting and using resources responsibly.
  • Reforestation means restoring trees in areas where forests were reduced or removed.
  • Sustainable use considers both present needs and the ability of ecosystems and resources to support the future.
H. Forests, Oceans, and Philippine Environment
  • Forests provide habitat, support food webs, help protect soil, and contribute to the water cycle.
  • Mangroves provide coastal habitat and can help protect shorelines.
  • Coral reefs are diverse marine ecosystems that provide habitat for many organisms.
  • Plastic and other waste can harm marine organisms and coastal ecosystems.
  • Protecting habitats is one of the most important ways to conserve biodiversity.
I. Climate, Atmosphere, Energy, and Water
  • Weather describes short-term atmospheric conditions; climate describes long-term patterns.
  • The greenhouse effect is a natural process involving heat-trapping gases in the atmosphere.
  • Global warming refers to the long-term rise in Earth's average surface temperature.
  • The ozone layer absorbs much of the Sun's harmful ultraviolet radiation.
  • Solar and wind energy are renewable energy sources; coal and petroleum are nonrenewable.
  • The water cycle includes evaporation, condensation, precipitation, runoff, and other movements of water.
